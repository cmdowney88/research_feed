# Research taste profile

Read by the triage step to assign tiers and to write "why this matters to you" lines, and by
the note writer to frame relevance. Write it in plain prose: the more specific, the better the
triage. Refine it as your ratings show where triage disagrees with you.

## Who

One paragraph: your field, the problems you work on, the settings (languages, data, domains),
and who you collaborate with.

## Core interests (ranked)

1. **Most important interest:** what exactly, which methods, which settings.
2. **Second interest:** ...
3. ...

## Flagged authors

Any paper by these authors gets **at least tier 3** (worth knowing it exists). Judge the tier
on content like any other paper. IDs are in `configs/feeds.yaml`.

## Methodological taste

- What kinds of papers you value (careful comparisons, simple baselines, new resources, ...).
- What you are skeptical of.

## Currently active (relevance boost)

- Projects you are working on right now; papers bearing on them rank higher.

## Likely tier 4

- Kinds of papers that look on-topic but that you don't want to see.

## Tiers

1. **Careful read:** directly relevant to an active project or a top interest, or a result you
   would want to cite or argue with.
2. **Skim:** relevant to a core interest but incremental.
3. **Worth knowing it exists, if nothing else:** useful for related-work coverage.
4. **Probably not interesting:** matches a tier-4 pattern or is only superficially on-topic.
   No notes.
